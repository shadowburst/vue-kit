# Member-cap enforcement on subscription downgrade

Tier-based seat caps (`Free = 0`, `Pro = 3` non-Owner Memberships per **Team**, configured under `tiers.{tier}.member_cap` in `config/billing.php`) are exposed as the integer-valued `Feature::TeamMemberCap` Pennant feature. `UserPolicy::create` combines `$user->can('user.create')` with `members.count(non-owner) < cap` so a single decision point governs invites. Cancellation is voluntary-through-our-controller (Stripe Customer Portal cancel disabled); subscriptions cancel at period end; on `customer.subscription.deleted` the same load-bearing webhook listener that purges Pennant cache (ADR-0008) detaches the most-recently-added non-Owner Memberships down to the new cap, ordered by `model_has_roles.created_at desc` (column added by migration). Involuntary cancellation (payment failure) skips the prune; the **Team** becomes over-cap-but-intact, only new invites blocked.

## Considered options

- **Strict no-action: cap blocks invites only; existing Memberships keep access indefinitely after downgrade.** Rejected because a Pro team with 3 non-Owners that downgrades to Free pays nothing yet retains full collaboration — the upgrade funnel ADR-0008 was designed to preserve evaporates as soon as anyone notices.
- **Block downgrade until under-cap.** Refuse to cancel the subscription if `members.count() > newCap`. Rejected because hostile cancel flows trigger chargebacks and breach the spirit of Stripe's frictionless-cancel guidance.
- **Soft-delete `model_has_roles` rows on downgrade with reactivation on resubscribe.** Considered for the data-preservation appeal. Rejected because the Spatie pivot is intentionally implicit (ADR-0001), has no timestamps, and Spatie's `HasRoles` queries plus its `PermissionRegistrar` cache don't honor pivot soft-deletes — adopting this would require either forking the trait or maintaining a parallel scoping layer that drifts on every Spatie upgrade.
- **Sidecar `team_member_lockouts` table preserving role assignments and supporting reactivation.** Considered as the safest compromise. Rejected because the explicit requirement was "delete N members," consent for the destruction comes from the in-app grace-period banner, and the reactivation rules a sidecar would enable (FIFO restore on resubscribe, ordering across multiple downgrade/upgrade cycles) introduce real complexity for behavior nobody asked for.
- **`current_team_id = null` as the lockout signal, cap measured on "active seats."** Rejected because the cap then measures "users currently looking at this team" rather than "members on the team," which is not what tier-based seat enforcement should mean and produces a musical-chairs UX where members race for seats by switching.
- **Hard-delete `model_has_roles` rows at period end via webhook; consent collected through a grace-period banner** (chosen). The Owner consents implicitly by not resuming during the grace window. The action is destructive but legible — and the Owner who needs the data preserved can always resubscribe before the period ends.

## Why

The tier system's goal is a *visible* upgrade lever, not a paper one. "Cap blocks invites only" leaves an obvious workaround (a 100-person team stays on Free forever as long as nobody invites a 101st), which guts the incentive. Hard-delete with grace period draws a clean line: while you pay, your team is intact; when you stop paying, the team contracts to what your tier supports.

Choosing to own the cancel button (hiding it in Stripe's Portal) is what makes the grace-period banner viable. If cancel could happen on Stripe-hosted UI without our knowledge, the warning would be ineffective and the only consistent fallback would be silent webhook auto-prune for *all* cancel paths — surfacing destruction with no human in the loop. Concentrating cancel through our controller restores that loop.

## Consequences

- `model_has_roles` gains a `created_at` column (the Spatie default migration omits all timestamps). No backfill — pre-production. Going forward, every Membership row is timestamped, which gives "latest N" a defined meaning. This is purely additive to ADR-0001's pivot — no separate membership table.
- `config/billing.php` adds `tiers.{tier}.member_cap` integer slots alongside the existing `monthly` / `yearly` Stripe Price IDs. `Feature::TeamMemberCap`'s closure reads it.
- `Feature::TeamMemberCap` is the first non-boolean Pennant feature in this codebase. The `auth.features` shared prop's `array<string, mixed>` typing (ADR-0008) accommodates this without change.
- `UserPolicy::create` is the second policy method to consult Pennant. Its `auth.abilities.user.create` value collapses both axes into one boolean for the disabled state; the frontend distinguishes failure modes by reading `auth.features.team-member-cap` and the current Membership count alongside.
- The `customer.subscription.deleted` webhook handler does two things now: purge Pennant cache (ADR-0008) **and** detach over-cap non-Owner Memberships ordered by `model_has_roles.created_at desc`. Both are idempotent. The handler's load-bearing status (ADR-0008) is reinforced — failure now has two consequences (stale tier *plus* unpruned over-cap Memberships) instead of one.
- The cancel button moves from Stripe's Customer Portal into our own controller. Stripe Portal config sets `features.subscription_cancel.enabled = false`. Payment-method update, invoice download, and other Portal features remain.
- Cancellation is period-end (Cashier's `cancel()` default). During the grace period, an Inertia shared-prop slot (`auth.subscription.grace_period`) carries the period-end timestamp and the count of Memberships that will be detached, used by a banner and a Resume button (`subscription->resume()`).
- Involuntary cancellation (payment failure → Stripe transitions to `unpaid` then cancelled) is distinguished from voluntary cancellation by the absence of an Owner-initiated grace period; the listener skips the prune. The Team becomes over-cap-but-intact; the Owner gets dunning email and can resubscribe to restore normal state. This is the only path where a Team can persist over-cap.
- Pricing is unchanged: ADR-0007's "flat-fee per team, no per-seat" still holds. The cap is a *capability* gate, not a billing dimension.
- `subscription.update` (the existing permission for managing subscription state) governs both Cancel and Resume actions. No new permission is introduced.
